01

Brand strategy

Every decision in this document is a consequence of the positioning below — not a matter of taste. If a design choice can't be traced back to it, it doesn't belong in the brand.

For operations leaders in wholesale and distribution who are tired of clunky, outdated software, Ledgerline is the ERP platform that feels as fast and clear as the business it runs — unlike legacy systems that slow teams down.

ReliableNever the flashiest thing in the room. Always the steadiest.
DirectSays what it means. No filler, no jargon.
ModernLooks like software built this decade, not ported from 2009.
HumanOne warm note in an otherwise cool, structured system.
02

Logo

The mark reads as three ledger lines that total into a rising tick — the moment a column of figures resolves into a confirmed number. It's literal enough to explain in one sentence, and abstract enough to work at 16px.

Stacked lockup — social avatars, square placements
Mark alone — once the wordmark is established elsewhere
App icon / favicon
Reversed — for navy or photographic backgrounds

Clear space

Keep space equal to the height of the mark itself free on all sides. Nothing else — text, other logos, edges of the page — enters that zone.

Don't rotate
Don't recolour
Don't restroke

Misuse

The mark's three strokes carry a fixed weight relationship and colour order. Changing any one breaks the "totalling" reading it's built on.

03

Colour

Navy carries the brand; amber is used deliberately, never decoratively. If amber appears more than once on a screen, that's a sign something else should be doing the work instead.

Ink Navy#17233F — primary, wordmark, dark UI

04

Typography

Space Grotesk carries the brand's confidence in headlines. IBM Plex Sans was designed for dense technical interfaces, not marketing pages — which matters here, because most of what this typeface will render is a screen full of stock counts and order lines, not a hero headline.

Everything that moves,
in one place.
Space Grotesk, 600 weight, tight tracking — used only for headlines and the wordmark. Never for body copy.
Space Grotesk — Display
Headlines, section titles, the logo
Weights used: 500 (wordmark), 600–700 (headlines). Set tight, often -0.01 to -0.02em tracking at large sizes.
IBM Plex Sans — Text & UI
Body copy, UI labels, data tables
Weights used: 400 (body), 500 (labels, buttons). Chosen for legibility at small sizes in dense screens.

05

Iconography & imagery

Icons stay structurally consistent with the logo's own construction: rounded line strokes, no fill, one weight. Imagery avoids generic corporate stock in favour of a treated, consistent duotone.

Duotone treatment

Warehouse and trade-floor photography treated in navy-to-amber duotone, so even a generic stock photo reads as on-brand. Never full-colour photography on marketing surfaces.

Subject matter

Real operational moments — stock being counted, orders being picked, a till or dashboard mid-use — rather than posed handshake-and-lanyard corporate stock. If a shot could be from any industry, it's the wrong shot.
